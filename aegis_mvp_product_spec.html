Product Vision, MVP Thesis & Scope

Project Aegis is a concierge digital-protection service for people who are targets by virtue of being known — celebrities, athletes, founders, creators, and their households. It is not antivirus and not a dashboard you're expected to operate. It is a standing operation: continuous AI/ML monitoring of the client's entire digital footprint, a human red-team that attacks the client before criminals do, a 24/7 human SOC concierge who is a name-and-a-face rather than a ticket queue, and a takedown war-room that collapses an incident from days to hours. The MVP thesis is that the wealthy already buy this posture physically (Gavin de Becker, executive protection details) but have nothing equivalent for the digital plane — and that BlackCloak, the closest analog, is exec-only, alert-heavy, and pre-AI. Aegis wins by being AI-native on volume and unapologetically human on crisis.

One-line definition & north-star

DEFINITION: Aegis is a 24/7 concierge service that protects a famous person's entire digital life — accounts, devices, identity, money, and household — using AI to watch continuously and humans to intervene when it counts.

NORTH-STAR: 'Our client learns about the attack from us — with it already handled — not from TMZ, not from a ransom DM, and never from a stranger holding their photos.' Operationally this is one number: Threat-Ahead Rate — the % of material incidents Aegis detects and contains before the client (or the public) becomes aware. MVP target: >=85%.

What 'top of the line' means concretely (not adjectives — thresholds)

  • Human answer, always: a named Protection Lead + SOC coverage 24/7/365 via a dedicated Signal line and a white-glove app. Live-human answer to a RED alert in <=90 seconds; median human acknowledgment of any concierge message in <5 minutes.
  • Detection latency measured in minutes, not scans: credential/PII exposure surfaced within 15 minutes of appearing in SpyCloud/Flare/DeHashed feeds; impersonation profiles and phishing domains within 30 minutes of first observation.
  • Takedown that actually lands: median malicious-domain takedown <6 hours; fraudulent social profile removal <12 hours, via pre-negotiated trusted-flagger channels (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, X) and registrar/host abuse pipelines (Netcraft/ZeroFox-class).
  • Offense on retainer: quarterly human red-team engagement per client — real OSINT dossier, real phishing simulation, real credential-stuffing attempt against the client's own perimeter — with a written 'here's how we'd have taken you' report.
  • Zero-knowledge posture: Aegis stores the minimum to protect you and, where it must hold secrets, holds them so that even Aegis staff and a subpoena-armed adversary cannot read them (client-held keys, enclave-only processing).
  • The household is covered by default: spouse, kids, chief of staff, and estate manager are first-class protected identities, because that is where attackers actually pivot in.

Design principles (the 6 rules the whole product obeys)

Concierge, not software
P0 · Launch

The product is a relationship, not a login. Default interaction is a human on Signal who already knows your context; the app exists to make the human faster, never to make you do security work. We measure success by how little the client has to touch it.

How Named Protection Lead per client; SOC runbooks in Tines/Torq; client app (React Native) is read-and-approve only — one-tap approvals via push, no config screens.
AI does the volume, humans do the crisis
P0 · Launch

Machines triage the 10,000 daily signals down to the handful that matter; humans own every decision that touches the client's name, money, or safety. No automated action with irreversible client impact ever fires without a human in the loop.

How ML/LLM triage layer (Claude-based classification + anomaly models) auto-dismisses noise and drafts the incident brief; a SOC analyst authorizes any client-facing action or takedown.
Zero-knowledge by default
P0 · Launch

Aegis is architected so that the most damaging data — vault secrets, recovery material, private media hashes — is never readable by Aegis. We can protect what we can't decrypt; that is the point.

How Client-controlled keys; secrets processing only inside AWS Nitro Enclaves; sensitive matching done on perceptual hashes/HMACs, not raw content; CloudHSM-backed key hierarchy.
The whole household is the perimeter
P0 · Launch

The client is rarely the weakest link — the 19-year-old with the reused password or the assistant who controls the calendar is. Protection is scoped to a 'Protected Circle,' not one VIP.

How Household graph model: each member is a monitored identity with their own footprint scan, MFA posture, and consent record; pivot-path analysis flags the softest entry point.
Discretion is a feature, not a promise
P0 · Launch

Our clients fear exposure by their protectors as much as by attackers. Discretion is enforced in architecture and access, not just in an NDA.

How Codename-per-client (no real names in tooling), need-to-know RBAC via Okta, full analyst-access audit trail, and a policy that no client is ever named in marketing without written opt-in.
Assume breach, rehearse the crisis
P0 · Launch

We treat compromise as inevitable and optimize for time-to-contain. The war-room and the client's response are pre-scripted, not improvised at 3 a.m.

How Per-client Incident Response Plan on file; quarterly tabletop; pre-authorized takedown and account-lockdown playbooks so the SOC executes on minute one, not after chasing approvals.
Every alert earns its interruption
P0 · Launch

The failure mode of BlackCloak-style tools is alert fatigue. Aegis never sends the client a raw alert — only human-verified, action-framed messages with the work mostly done.

How Severity model (RED/AMBER/GREEN); client only ever sees RED (act now) and curated weekly GREEN digest; AMBER is handled silently by the SOC.

The single hardest problem: Aegis becomes the juiciest honeypot on Earth

The moment Aegis exists, it aggregates the digital keys, recovery paths, and private media context of the most-targeted people alive. That concentration is a nation-state-grade magnet — a single breach of Aegis would be a civilization-scale Celebgate, and it would end the company. Every architecture decision in the MVP is bent around making an Aegis breach worth as little as possible to the attacker.

HOW THE MVP IS ARCHITECTED AROUND IT: (1) Zero-knowledge by construction — we do not hold plaintext of what we protect; client-controlled keys and CloudHSM-backed hierarchies mean a full database exfil yields ciphertext, not photos or passwords. (2) Enclave-only processing — any operation that must touch a secret runs inside AWS Nitro Enclaves with attestation; no human and no persistent host ever sees the cleartext. (3) Hashes, not content — impersonation and leaked-media matching operate on perceptual hashes / HMACs, so 'we know your photo leaked' never requires us to store your photo. (4) Compartmentalization — codename-per-client, per-client key domains, and strict need-to-know RBAC mean compromising one analyst or one client's context cannot cascade across the book. (5) Minimize the blast radius — we deliberately do NOT custody assets, host the client's password vault, or run their IdP in the MVP; the less we hold, the less we are worth stealing. The discipline is simple and non-negotiable: if losing it would be catastrophic, Aegis should never be able to read it in the first place.

The Panic Button — One-Tap Lockdown & Named-Human Response

The Panic Button is the emotional core of Aegis and the demo that closes the sale. It collapses the worst moment of a client's life — "someone is in my accounts right now" — into a single action, available three ways: an in-app SOS, a memorized phone hotline, and an optional physical trigger. The instant it fires, two things happen in parallel: an automated fan-out force-logs-out every session, revokes OAuth grants, rotates critical credentials, freezes account changes, and remote-locks devices; and a named Aegis Protector — the specific human already briefed on the client's full asset map — answers voice in under 60 seconds. It is not a support ticket. It is a bodyguard tackling the threat while calling your name.

Three ways to pull the trigger

Panic never arrives on schedule, so the button meets the client wherever the panic is.

1. In-app SOS. A persistent red shield on the Aegis home screen and in the iOS/Android home-screen widget and Lock Screen widget (iOS 16+ WidgetKit). Press-and-hold 800ms (deliberate, un-pocket-dialable) or triple-tap. A haptic thud confirms. No login required if the device is already enrolled — the app uses a Secure Enclave-backed device key so a locked-out client whose password was just changed can still fire.

2. The Hotline. A dedicated, memorized number (vanity DID provisioned via Twilio, e.g. +1-855-AEGIS-11) routed through Twilio Voice with <2-ring pickup. Caller ID is matched against the client roster; the IVR is skipped entirely for known clients and the call lands directly on the on-shift Protector's softphone. Saying the client's duress passphrase escalates to a physical-safety protocol (loops in the client's real-world security detail / law enforcement liaison).

3. Physical trigger (P1). A pairable Flic 2 Bluetooth button or the client's Apple Watch (WatchOS complication + Action Button on Ultra) for the person whose phone is the thing that just got stolen. Double-click fires the identical fan-out over BLE-to-phone or cellular Watch.

All three converge on the same event: a single panic.triggered event on the Aegis backend that stamps client ID, trigger source, geolocation, and device fingerprint, then fires the orchestrated response.

The first 60 seconds — incident flow

  1. 01
    T+0s — Trigger fires

    Client presses SOS / calls hotline / clicks physical button. Backend receives signed panic.triggered event. App screen flips to the calm dark 'Lockdown Active' state (see in-app copy). A PagerDuty high-urgency incident is opened and the named Protector's phone rings simultaneously with the automated fan-out — response and containment run in parallel, never sequentially.

  2. 02
    T+0–5s — Session kill begins

    Orchestrator (a durable Temporal workflow, so no step is lost mid-fan-out) issues parallel revocation calls: Google Admin SDK Directory API users.signOut + reset of OAuth tokens; Apple session invalidation via the client's pre-authorized Managed Apple ID (Apple Business Manager) and App-Specific Password teardown; Meta/Instagram Graph API + me/permissions DELETE to drop all connected apps; Microsoft Graph revokeSignInSessions.

  3. 03
    T+5–15s — OAuth & connected-app purge

    Every third-party OAuth grant the client authorized (the classic Celebgate vector — a shady 'photo backup' app with standing token access) is revoked across Google, Microsoft, Meta, X, and Dropbox. Standing refresh tokens are the real threat, so these are killed before passwords — a rotated password does nothing if an attacker holds a live refresh token.

  4. 04
    T+15–30s — Credential rotation

    Aegis-managed vault (1Password Connect / Bitwarden org API) auto-rotates the 'critical tier' credentials tagged in onboarding: primary email, Apple ID, Google, Instagram, primary bank/brokerage, and any crypto exchange (Coinbase/Kraken). New high-entropy passwords are written to the vault; the client never sees or needs them. Where supported, TOTP seeds are re-provisioned and the attacker's authenticator is orphaned.

  5. 05
    T+15–40s — Freeze & device lock

    Account-change freeze: email-forwarding rules, recovery-phone/recovery-email changes, and API key creation are locked or reverted via each platform's admin API (attackers set forwarding rules and swap recovery info to re-establish persistence). In parallel, MDM (Jamf Pro / Kandji) issues DeviceLock + EraseDevice-armed commands to enrolled iPhone/iPad/Mac; lost devices are put in Lost Mode with a callback number.

SIM-Swap Kill & Crown-Jewel Account Hardening

Ninety percent of high-profile account takeovers — Celebgate, the crypto drains, the hijacked verified handles — trace back to two primitives: a phone number that can be ported to an attacker's SIM, and an account-recovery flow that trusts either that number or "security questions" whose answers are printed in Wikipedia. Aegis's first hardened feature kills both. In a single white-glove onboarding session (60-90 minutes, in person or over an encrypted concierge call) we (1) lock the client's carrier line against port-out, (2) migrate every account off SMS one-time-passcodes onto FIDO2 passkeys and pre-provisioned YubiKeys, (3) stand up a hardened, unpublished recovery number on a separate carrier, and (4) rebuild account recovery so it no longer depends on public facts. The client's involvement is minimal: they tap a key when we tell them to. Aegis does the rest. This is the pragmatic, "genuinely better than Norton/BlackCloak" core — not a scanner that emails you a PDF, but an operator who removes the attack surface.

The kill chain we're breaking

A SIM swap is not sophisticated. An attacker calls the carrier (or bribes a $14/hr retail rep), impersonates the client using a birthday and a former address scraped in ten minutes, and moves the number to a SIM in their pocket. Every SMS code the client's bank, email, exchange, and Instagram sends now lands on the attacker's phone. From there, 'Forgot password → text me a code' unlocks everything in sequence. The entire chain rests on one assumption: possession of the phone number equals identity. Aegis's job is to make that assumption false — first by making the number un-portable, then by making sure that even a stolen number unlocks nothing, because nothing valuable still trusts SMS. We treat the phone number as radioactive: contained, monitored, and removed from every load-bearing role.

The Recovery Paradox — and how we fix it

A celebrity's knowledge-based-authentication (KBA) answers are public forever. Their mother's maiden name is on a genealogy site, their first pet is in a 2016 profile, their high school is on Wikipedia, their birthday trends on X every year. You cannot 'reset' a public fact. So Aegis stops treating facts as secrets. For every account, we (1) delete or neutralize KBA where the platform allows it; (2) where security questions are mandatory, we answer them with high-entropy fictional strings generated by the vault (e.g. 'First pet?' → 'gx7$Lm-Quokka-Vault-4471') and store them like passwords — the true answer becomes a secret only Aegis and the client hold, not a Google search; (3) we replace 'recovery = phone/email + KBA' with cryptographic and human recovery: iCloud/Google Recovery Contacts pointed at an Aegis-controlled hardened identity, FIDO2 keys held in escrow, and a manual carrier/platform recovery that routes through Aegis's SOC with out-of-band voice verification and a client-specific safe-word. The result: there is no self-service recovery path an attacker can walk, because the only recovery path runs through people who know the client and hardware the attacker can't hold.

Always-On Monitoring & Threat Detection

Aegis Sentinel is the always-on sensing layer of Project Aegis — a fusion of streaming ML anomaly detection, dark-web and breach intelligence, impersonation/deepfake hunting, and continuous attack-surface reconnaissance, all resolved to a single client identity graph. Architecture in one breath

Every surface below emits events into a single Confluent Kafka bus. Apache Flink jobs enrich each event against the client's Identity Graph (a Neo4j graph of the principal, household, staff, entities, known devices, home/office IPs, travel calendar, and verified social handles) and score it 0–100 with a gradient-boosted model (XGBoost) plus rule overlays. Sub-threshold events accrete silently on the client's risk timeline. The design goal: the AI does 95% of the looking; a human does 100% of the deciding on anything that reaches the client.

The Entourage Graph — Why It's Architecturally Different

  • Household-as-org, not account-as-silo: competitors protect one exec's inbox. We model the principal, family, and staff as a single governed tenant where the real attack path — 'the assistant's laptop has the password to everything' — is finally visible and controllable.
  • Least-privilege by construction: every seat is provisioned from a role template with an explicit scope; over-provisioning is a scored penalty in the Aegis Score, so hygiene is enforced, not hoped for.
  • Per-seat vault scopes on 1Password Business: vaults map to sensitivity tiers (Crown Jewels / Financial / Household / Brand / IoT), groups map to roles, so the EA literally cannot open the crypto vault — it's not a policy, it's a permission.
  • The staff churn problem is the killer feature: assistants, nannies, and estate staff turn over constantly, and each departure is a live credential-exposure event. Instant offboarding turns a multi-day panic ('what did they have access to?') into a 60-second, audited action.
  • Immutable audit trail per tenant: every access grant, vault open, and offboarding is append-only logged — the artifact a family office's lawyers and insurers actually want to see.
  • Delegated, revocable, time-boxable access: a contractor stylist can get a 72-hour scoped grant that auto-expires, instead of a permanent shared login that outlives the engagement.

The AI & ML Engine (MVP)

The Aegis engine is a streaming, always-on detection layer that fuses six purpose-built pipelines behind a single LLM analyst copilot and a cross-client threat graph. We buy commodity detection (deepfake audio, reverse-image, breach data) and build the parts that are our moat (behavioral scoring, message triage tuned to a specific principal, and the graph that turns one client's incident into cohort-wide immunity). Every high-severity inference is human-in-the-loop through our 24/7 SOC — the AI never acts unilaterally on a takedown or an account lockout. The MVP target is sub-60-second detection-to-alert on account-takeover signals and a managed false-positive rate that keeps concierge escalations to what a human analyst can lovingly handle.

Architecture at a glance

Everything lands on a Kafka (Confluent Cloud) event bus and is scored in a Flink streaming layer, with feature state in Redis and a Feast feature store on top of Snowflake for training. Model serving runs on AWS (SageMaker endpoints for our own models, Bedrock for hosted Claude/LLM inference). Detections above threshold create an Incident object in the SOC console, where the LLM copilot pre-triages and a human analyst owns the decision. We deliberately keep the principal's most sensitive data (message bodies, face vectors) in a per-client encrypted enclave (AWS Nitro Enclaves, KMS per-tenant keys) — models read features, not raw content, wherever possible.

1. Login & Behavioral Anomaly Detection

Signal sources: OAuth/session telemetry from the client's Google, Apple, Microsoft 365, Meta, and X accounts (via their security APIs and, where needed, our managed-account model), plus a lightweight endpoint agent on the principal's and key staff's devices. Features (~60): geo/IP reputation (MaxMind + IPQualityScore), ASN, impossible-travel velocity, device fingerprint delta, TLS/JA3 fingerprint, time-of-day vs. the principal's learned rhythm, new-OAuth-grant events, MFA-method changes, and 'dormant device woke up' flags. Model class: a gradient-boosted tree (XGBoost/LightGBM) producing a calibrated risk score, wrapped by a deterministic rules layer for the un-missable stuff (e.g., 'new device + password change + email-forwarding rule added within 10 min' fires regardless of the ML score). We start with a rules-heavy cold-start per client and let the GBM take over as ~2-4 weeks of per-principal baseline accrues. Streaming inference in Flink; p95 detection-to-alert under 60 seconds. False-positive control: personalized per-client thresholds, a 'known travel' calendar the concierge can pre-load, and a two-tier severity so a medium anomaly becomes a silent log entry, not a 3am phone call.

2. Phishing / Scam-Message Classifier

This screens inbound email, SMS (via a managed number / iMessage relay where the client opts in), and social DMs (Instagram, X) for the principal and their inner circle — the exact vector that took down the 2014 Celebgate victims. Pipeline: cheap heuristics first (SPF/DKIM/DMARC failures, lookalike/homograph domains via dnstwist, URL detonation in a sandbox — urlscan.io + our own headless-browser detonator, known-bad hashes), then a fine-tuned LLM classifier (Claude via Bedrock, plus a distilled DeBERTa-v3 model for high-volume cheap first-pass) that scores intent: credential phish, extortion/sextortion, fake-brand-deal, crypto/investment scam, or benign. We fine-tune on public phishing corpora (APWG, PhishTank), our red-team's generated lures, and — critically — labels flowing back from the SOC. Output is a risk band, not auto-deletion: high-risk items are quarantined and surfaced to the concierge; the principal sees a clean inbox. Human-in-the-loop on anything that would touch the principal's real correspondence. FP management: allowlist of the client's real agents/managers/brands, per-client tuning, and a 'when in doubt, a human reads it' floor — discretion over automation.

3. Impersonation Detection

Continuously hunts fake profiles across Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook, YouTube, and Telegram. Method: we hold reference embeddings of the principal's real face (a face-recognition model — ArcFace/InsightFace) and profile/brand assets (CLIP image embeddings), then scan new and existing accounts for handles that are lookalikes (edit-distance + homoglyph matching on the verified handle), profile photos that are near-duplicates or face matches, and bios that scrape the principal's real copy. We combine platform search APIs, a licensed social-listening feed (e.g., Brandwatch/Bright Data managed collection), and reverse-image search (Google Vision + TinEye API) to find where the principal's photos are being reused. A match above a cosine-similarity threshold (tuned per platform, typically ~0.6 CLIP / ~0.4 ArcFace distance) creates an impersonation candidate. Human-in-the-loop: an analyst confirms before any takedown notice goes out — a verified fan account is not a threat. FP control: verified/blue-check exclusion, the client's own known alt-accounts allowlisted, and clustering so 400 scraper-bot clones collapse into one incident.

4. Deepfake & Voice-Clone Detection

Explicit build-vs-buy call: we BUY detection here in the MVP and wrap it in our workflow. We integrate a specialist vendor API (e.g., Reality Defender and/or Hive AI for image/video/audio deepfake scoring, with Pindrop as an option for voice-liveness on phone channels) rather than trying to out-research a research problem in six months. Trigger paths: (a) monitored media where the principal's face/voice appears gets auto-scored; (b) the concierge can submit a suspect clip ('is this really our client?') and get a confidence score back in minutes; (c) inbound voice calls to protected staff lines can be screened for synthetic-voice markers. Human-in-the-loop is mandatory — a deepfake determination is a reputational grenade, so a human analyst plus, for high-stakes cases, a documented second opinion. We log vendor confidence and our own disposition to build a proprietary eval set, which is how a future build decision gets de-risked.

5. LLM Analyst Copilot

A Claude-powered agent (via Bedrock, with tool use / function calling) that sits inside the SOC console and does the un-glamorous 80%: it auto-triages every incoming detection (dedupes, correlates related signals across pipelines, assigns a proposed severity with a one-paragraph rationale and cited evidence), drafts platform-specific takedown notices and DMCA / impersonation reports pre-filled with the evidence bundle, and writes the client-facing incident summary in plain, calm, non-technical language. Security & Privacy Architecture — Zero-Knowledge by Design

Aegis holds the most valuable secrets a famous person owns: the passwords to their iCloud, the seed phrase to their cold wallet, the recovery codes to the accounts that ARE their career. So we built Aegis on a single non-negotiable premise — Aegis servers must be technically incapable of reading client plaintext. Credentials and secrets are encrypted on the client with keys derived from a passphrase the client alone knows; our servers store only opaque ciphertext they can never decrypt. Every sensitive recovery action is gated behind split-knowledge M-of-N approval. And the hardest part of our threat model isn't the outside attacker — it's us. This section is the architecture that lets us look a paparazzi-hunted client in the eye and say: even under subpoena, even fully breached, even with a rogue employee, there is nothing here for anyone to steal.

The Zero-Knowledge Vault: keys never leave the client

The Aegis Vault is a true end-to-end-encrypted store, modeled on the proven architecture of 1Password's Secret Key + Bitwarden's zero-knowledge design, and then hardened. When a client onboards, their device generates a random 256-bit account secret and combines it with their master passphrase. We stretch that material with Argon2id (memory-hard: 512 MiB, t=4, p=2 — tuned to make GPU/ASIC cracking economically absurd) to derive a symmetric master key, entirely in the client (native iOS/Android app + a WASM crypto core in the browser, built on libsodium). Individual vault items are sealed with per-item keys using XChaCha20-Poly1305 (AEAD) wrapped by the master key; a per-user Curve25519 keypair enables secure sharing to the concierge SOC when — and only when — the client explicitly authorizes it. The plaintext, the master passphrase, and the derived master key NEVER transit to or land on Aegis infrastructure. Our servers receive a blob that is, to us, indistinguishable from random noise. There is no 'admin decrypt' button, no master override, no support-side reset that reveals contents — because none can exist without breaking the math. If a client loses their passphrase, the encrypted data is gone; recovery runs exclusively through the M-of-N social-recovery path below, never through an Aegis backdoor.

Split-knowledge M-of-N: no single human (client OR Aegis) can act alone

  • Any sensitive recovery action — vault key recovery, wallet seed reconstruction, or a break-glass credential retrieval during a live incident — is gated by Shamir's Secret Sharing (SSS). Recovery secrets are split into N shares with a threshold M required to reconstruct (default 3-of-5).
  • Shares are distributed across independent trust domains: (1) the client's own device, (2) a client-nominated trusted person (family office, attorney, spouse), (3) an Aegis-held share sealed inside an HSM, (4) an offline paper/steel-plate share in the client's physical possession, (5) an escrow share held by an independent third-party fiduciary. No single domain — including Aegis — holds enough to reconstruct anything.
  • Reconstruction happens client-side inside a hardened enclave; Aegis's single share is mathematically useless in isolation. Even a total compromise of Aegis + a court order for our share yields exactly one share — below threshold, therefore zero information.
  • For high-consequence actions (crypto seed access, disabling MFA on a client's behalf), Aegis enforces dual-control on OUR side too: two named senior SOC operators must independently approve, each with hardware-key (YubiKey / WebAuthn) step-up, and the client must confirm out-of-band via a pre-registered voice/passphrase challenge to defeat social-engineering and deepfake-voice attacks.
  • Every share operation is logged to an immutable, append-only ledger (see insider-threat controls) with the specific quorum members and timestamps.

Tenant isolation, HSM & KMS

  • Per-tenant cryptographic isolation: every client gets a dedicated AWS KMS customer-managed key (CMK). A breach of one tenant's key exposes exactly one tenant — never the book. Highest-tier clients get a dedicated CloudHSM partition and can hold veto (key-approval) rights over their own CMK usage.
  • Silo isolation model (not pooled): sensitive stores are partitioned per tenant with row-level + key-level separation, so a query bug can't leak across clients. Compute for recovery/reconstruction runs inside AWS Nitro Enclaves with cryptographic attestation — even Aegis engineers with host root cannot read enclave memory.
  • AWS CloudHSM provides FIPS 140-2 Level 3 hardware-backed key custody for the Aegis-held recovery shares and root secrets. Keys are non-exportable; all cryptographic operations occur inside the HSM boundary.
  • Envelope encryption everywhere: data keys wrapped by per-tenant CMKs wrapped by HSM-rooted master keys. Automatic key rotation (annual for CMKs, on-demand for incident response). TLS 1.3 in transit; mTLS between internal services.
  • Infrastructure isolation: production is a locked-down AWS account with SCP guardrails, no direct human SSH (all access brokered through Teleport with session recording), and IaC-only changes via peer-reviewed Terraform.

Building Trust Fast in a Reputation-Driven Market

In this business you don't earn trust by claiming it — you earn it by being provably more secure than the people you protect and by being invisible. Four moves, fast: (1) Close SOC 2 Type I by Month 6 and start Type II immediately — a business manager's lawyer will ask, and 'in progress' loses the deal. (2) Adopt a zero-knowledge posture where possible: we monitor for exposure without hoarding the principal's secrets; passwords are rotated to values we never store, MFA re-anchors to the principal's own hardware keys and passkeys. Our breach is not their breach. (3) Weaponize proof over promises: the free Exposure Report and the quarterly red-team posture report turn 'trust us' into 'here is exactly what we found and fixed.' (4) Make discretion a contractual, cultural, and architectural default — mutual NDAs, no name-dropping ever, per-tenant data isolation, and analysts under strict need-to-know. The company that could go on stage and name its clients is the company that shouldn't have them. Ours never will.
